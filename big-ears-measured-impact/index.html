§ 01The problem the posting names

Most AI transformations report activity. Yours has to report impact.

The standard AI program counts what it did: tools licensed, sessions run, seats provisioned, a satisfaction score at the end. Those numbers rise whether or not a single person's work got better, which is why so many transformations feel busy and land soft. Your posting asks for something harder and more specific: the organization's first AI adoption and impact measurement framework. First means there is no baseline to inherit and no scoreboard to defend, which is the best possible starting condition and the reason the sequence matters.

The posting also names the trap in the same breath, and I would take it literally: map what is actually happening against what leadership believes is happening. A measurement framework built from the top measures the belief. Built from the ground, it measures the work, and the distance between those two readings is the most valuable thing the first ninety days can produce.

Listening is the input. Impact is the output. A transformation that only listens is a tour, and one that only measures is a scoreboard.
What follows is the loop that connects them, in the four movements your posting already describes.
§ 02The loop, in four movements
Movement 01 · Intake

Listen in, and out

Big ears. Map what is actually happening versus what leadership believes is happening.

Two intakes, running always. Inward: what each team does by hand today, where the workarounds are, what they already tried and abandoned. Outward: what became possible this quarter and what is about to be. A global platform needs both read across geographies, because the constraint in one region is rarely the constraint in another.

I have run the inward half as a discipline, not a survey. At eBay I studied how buyers and sellers felt about negotiation before designing it; at Yahoo! I led PM teams across Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the US, where teams disagree differently and need different kinds of context.

Produces: the gap map, refreshed, never finished
Movement 02 · Judgment

Rank by impact, not by enthusiasm

Build and maintain an iterative roadmap of AI transformation initiatives across and within our teams.

The gap map is a list of candidates, not a roadmap. Each one gets sized the same way: whose work does this change, how much of it, how would we know, and what does it cost to find out. The initiatives that survive are the ones tied to a business goal someone already owns.

This is business-case work, and it is how I have always earned budget. At Atlassian I won funding across four fronts through cases rather than headcount, including outside consultancy to pressure-test a replatform decision before we spent on it.

Produces: the iterative roadmap, and the reasons behind its order
Movement 03 · Proof

Stand up the measurement

Stand up the org's first AI adoption and impact measurement framework.

Adoption tells us what people tried. Impact tells us what changed: hours returned to mission work, cycle time on a campaign, quality holding steady while volume rises. Instrument before the pilot rather than after, so the pilot can answer a question instead of producing a story.

My metrics land on outcomes rather than usage curves. At Atlassian, users engaged in community were 7x more likely to repurchase, and I embedded experimentation frameworks org-wide so teams could measure their own outcomes without me in the room.

Produces: one instrumented scoreboard the exec forums can trust
Movement 04 · Spread

Teach what the evidence proved

A robust AI learning program: step-change training, frequent demos relevant to each team.

Curriculum built from the roadmap is guesswork. Curriculum built from movement three teaches what has already worked here, on this platform, for people with this job. Demos show a colleague's real result rather than a vendor's. Workshops turn one team's implementation into another team's starting point.

I run a version of this in public now: the AI Literacy Library at AIGal.io, a published methodology, and a live simulation. At Atlassian the same instinct ran as a champions network with a small core and embedded reach, which won CMX Community of the Year twice.

Produces: capability that compounds, and the next round of listening
The output of movement four is the input to movement one
§ 03What the loop puts on the calendar

A steady flow, not a launch.

Insight decays. A transformation that arrives as an annual plan is out of date by the time it is socialized, which is why the posting asks for frequent insight and regular step-change training rather than a program. The operating cadence is the deliverable, and it is what the AI-Exec, AI-PD, and working-group forums run on.

Every two weeks
The demo. One team shows what they actually shipped with AI, including what failed. Ten minutes on what became newly possible since the last one, translated per function rather than announced in general.
Monthly
The impact read. Adoption and impact side by side, published rather than presented, so a division lead can see their own numbers without asking. Where the two disagree becomes next month's listening.
Quarterly
Step change. The capability that materially moved, taught hands-on, with each function leaving having done the thing rather than watched it. Paired with a candid read on where adoption actually stands.
Always on
The pilot board. What is being experimented with, by whom, what we learned, and what we killed. Visible to everyone, because a transformation that hides its dead ends teaches people to hide theirs.

None of this requires headcount. It requires a cadence somebody owns, evidence worth showing, and the standing to tell an executive forum what the ground actually says. That last part is the job.

The close
An organization whose product is voice should run its own transformation by listening.

Change.org exists so that people can be heard by the institutions that affect them. The same principle, turned inward, is a transformation designed from what colleagues actually say and measured by whether their work got better. Collaboration isn't neutral. Design sets the trajectory.
