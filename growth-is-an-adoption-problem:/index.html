§ 01The problem the posting names

Interest is cheap. Habit is earned.

Every AI product gets its first week free: curiosity fills the funnel without anyone earning it. Past general availability the curve flattens, and the standard growth playbook reaches for funnel mechanics: shorten the signup, move the button, send the nudge. Useful, and not the problem. An AI agent asks users for the hardest thing in software: delegate a piece of your own work and trust what comes back. The conversion event is a behavior change. Everything else is staging for it.

I have launched behavior change at platform scale twice. Negotiation on a marketplace built on auctions and fixed prices moved $1B+ in its first year. Community and self-help at Atlassian turned engagement into repurchase across 78M+ monthly active users. Neither grew because a funnel was optimized. They grew because the product kept removing the reasons people hesitated, in the moments they hesitated.

Point AI at volume and it floods us. Design it for depth and our impact compounds.
For an agent, depth is the growth strategy: repeat delegation is the only signal that the behavior actually changed. That is the thesis of everything below.
§ 02The record, mapped to the growth loop
The mandate
Onboarding & activation
Drive new customer onboarding and activation; remove blockers across onboarding, admin setup, education, and permissions; improve the first-time experience.
The record

At Atlassian I led the self-help and onboarding platforms for 78M+ MAU: systems whose entire job was removing the reasons people stall. The platform primitives that came out of that work, onboarding flows and self-service tooling, were adopted by teams outside my reporting line for the most durable reason there is: they moved those teams' own activation numbers.

The mandate
Workflow discovery
Remove adoption blockers across workflow discovery and integrations; meet users inside the work they already do.
The record

Discovery is a distribution problem inside your own product, and I learned it the hard way. eBay's Best Offer was approved and funded; its visibility was not. Every placement and call to action lived on a surface owned by another product team, the seller's listing page, the buyer's flow, and was won owner by owner with user research and conversion projections, against a company deeply loyal to its auction format. $1B+ in transactions in year one. Patented.

The mandate
Retention & paid expansion
Drive retention, engagement, repeat usage, and paid expansion; define usage loops and expansion signals.
The record

My proudest metric is denominated in this posting's currency: users engaged in community were 7x more likely to repurchase. Community-led growth is a usage loop that ends at the P&L, not at a session count. Earlier, at Yahoo!, I took a 0→1 UGC platform from concept to 14M users and $40M revenue in year one: early traction to monetization inside twelve months.

The mandate
Measurement & experimentation
Define growth funnels, cohorts, activation and retention metrics; build experimentation roadmaps; decide from data.
The record

I embedded experimentation frameworks, A/B, multivariate, and funnel, org-wide at Atlassian, so teams could measure their own outcomes without me in the room. The discipline I hold: adoption tells us what users tried; outcomes tell us what stuck. Growth metrics should land on revenue, and mine do.

An AI agent is the first product that can run its own activation loop. It sits inside the workflow, sees the coordination it was never invited into, and can demonstrate the delegation right there, in flow, at the moment of hesitation. Concretely: the agent's second job, on every task, is teaching the user what to hand it next. I published this pattern as the always-on operator: AI that measures, contextualizes, and surfaces the next opportunity while humans decide. Growth for an agent is not a campaign around the product. It is a capability inside it.

Trust is the activation gate. People do not delegate to what they cannot verify, so the first-time experience has to show its work: actions previewed, sources visible, outcomes measured. My published rule for this is AI surfaces. Humans conclude. An agent that earns verification early gets handed bigger work later, and that handoff curve is the real expansion signal.

And from my published work on products past their feature-race phase: the next chapter is not shipping more, it is aiming. Adoption and retention metrics over feature counts, the highest-leverage segments first, and expansion following visible proof of recovered time rather than preceding it.

The close
Curiosity starts the trial. Habit signs the renewal.
