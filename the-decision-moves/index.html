Act II · The X-ray

This moment is two decades in the making.

The interface looks new. The problems underneath it are not: trust, discovery, attribution, handoffs, community intelligence, and where the decision actually lives.

Era 01
Marketplace
eBay · 2004–2005
The interface created trust between strangers.
Find→Inspect→ Compare→Decide→Transact
Community’s seatFeedback scores made strangers legible to one another. Reputation was the product.
Seen firsthand

Best Offer · negotiation inside a trust-based marketplace · $1B+ in transactions in year one.

Era 02
Search
Yahoo! Shopping · 2006–2010
The interface began routing intent — and value crossed a handoff.
Query→Compare→ Click→Merchant→ Decide→Transact
Community’s seatRatings and reviews became ranking inputs. The crowd started routing intent.
Seen firsthand

CPC → CPA · attribution across third-party checkout · cookie stomping · SEO reshaping discovery.

Era 03
Contextual commerce
Condé Nast + consulting · 2010–2018
Discovery moved away from the store. Transaction moved closer to intent.
Content→Inspiration→ Product→Decide→Transact
Community’s seatEditors, experts and creators became the discovery layer. Trust moved to people.
Seen firsthand

Early dynamic checkout experiments with Skimlinks and TwoTap: bring transaction closer to the moment of discovery.

Those interfaces moved the shopper through the decision.
The next one can enter the decision with them.

Act IV · The delegation shift

The steps did not disappear.

The decision moves · Delegation shift

For decades, commerce product teams optimized every step of a journey the human had to take.

Remove friction. Improve conversion.

Human
AI

The same six steps. One operator.

There is no universal setting for agency.
If the human no longer has to take every step, which steps should AI take — and who decides?
Seen firsthand

Two years intentionally designing and testing Human-AI collaboration. Across models, workflows, products, prototypes and real-world scenarios.

The boundary moves.
The product challenge is not to maximize autonomy. It is to help each person place the boundary intentionally — and move it as trust, context, and stakes change.

Human judgment determines the boundary.

Less delegationMore delegation
Person · Task · Context · Stakes · Trust

There isn’t a correct amount of AI.

There’s an appropriate allocation for this person, this task, this context and these stakes.

The decision boundary
Surface→ Narrow→ Recommend→ Prepare→ Act

AI can travel the whole chain. The human decides where the boundary sits — what to delegate, and where to stay in the decision — and accountability stays human.

The third intelligence

AI can compress the crowd. It shouldn’t erase the crowd.

Shopping has never been only shopper and merchant. Other people are part of the decision architecture too.

01 · AI compresses the crowd
“Customers generally love this vacuum.”
02 · The crowd contains important differences

owner reviews · long-term users · repair forums · expert tests · pet owners · community Q&A · creator reviews · contradictory experience

The useful signal may be the minority one: heavy pet-hair users consistently describe a failure mode that the average rating hides.

03 · Good compression preserves the difference
“Most owners rate it highly. Heavy pet-hair users are the important exception. Here’s what they report — and why it matters for you.”
Collective intelligence should become more usable — not more invisible.

Community is not an appendix to shopping. Reputation, reviews, lived experience, expertise, and dissent are trust infrastructure. The AI layer now has to surface them without flattening them into false consensus.

The shift
Shopping interfaces used to help us find the decision.
Now the interface can help make it.

Every time more of the shopping journey moved elsewhere, retailers adapted to protect the customer relationship. Agentic shopping creates the next adaptation problem.

The shopper’s intent can now generate actions the shopper never personally took.

The product agenda

Four questions that decide the shape of the system.

These aren’t questions to answer after the system is built. They’re questions the team has to answer before the answers harden into systems.

Progress

What happens when “bot traffic” goes from noise to one of our strongest signals of customer intent?

Handoff

How do we know when a handoff between human and AI worked?

Acting · Asking · Challenging · Returning control · Executing
Attribution + observability

How do we connect intent, influence and outcome when the journey moves across humans, agents and retailers?

If a review or creator changes an agent’s recommendation without the shopper ever seeing the source, how is that influence recognized — or attributed?

Optimization

What happens when the optimization isn’t to remove the step — but to move it?

And underneath all of them: how does the system earn — and keep earning — the right to more of the journey?

The funnel measured how humans moved. The next system has to measure how decisions move.
