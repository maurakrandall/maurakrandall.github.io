Adoption is the product.

A point of view on owning adoption and realized value as a product, from a leader who has spent 25 years accountable for experiences her teams did not fully own, and who has published the argument this role is built on.
"Building capable platforms is necessary but not sufficient; value is created only when colleagues adopt the governed path and change how they work. This role is accountable for that outcome." From the posting

§ 01The problem the posting names

No one owns the whole. That is the job.

Most enterprises write adoption roles as training functions with a dashboard attached. This posting does something rarer: it makes adoption a product, with an owner, a roadmap, and a P&L-grade definition of success. Someone at Pfizer has already won the argument I have been publishing for two years.

The posting is precise about why the role exists: a colleague's work moves across Loom, MemX, and Forge49 in a single workflow, and no individual platform team owns the end-to-end experience. I published the same diagnosis this year about another enterprise's platform estate: not one experience, but seven experiences bolted together by the employee's own patience.

The colleague should never be the API between the platforms. And the API should never be the end state.
In other words: stop making people do the plumbing's job, and don't mistake the plumbing for the product.

The hard part of this role is the part the posting states plainly: accountable for the experience, the adoption, and the realized value, without owning the teams that build the pieces. That tension is not a flaw in the design. It is the design, and it is the through-line of my career.

§ 02The record, mapped to the four functions
The mandate
Cross-platform product management
Own the experience that spans platforms this role does not manage, through influence and a credible prioritization mandate.
The record

At eBay, Best Offer was an approved, funded 0→1 project. Its success was not. A new buyer and seller platform lives or dies on visibility across the end-to-end experience, and none of that came free with approval: every inclusion, every call to action was negotiated with the team that owned the surface, from placement on the seller's listing page to inform, educate, and entice sellers, to the Make Best Offer prompt in the buyer's flow.

Those owners were deeply committed to eBay's auction format, and weak placement alone could have sunk the product. The only path was evidence: user research and projections on conversion and buyer and seller engagement, won surface by surface. $1B+ in transactions in year one. Patented.

At Yahoo!, I led the horizontal PM team shipping shared engagement components across verticals that did not have to adopt them. The real product was the culture change, moving teams from custom builds to shared components and APIs, and that shift came from my team.

The mandate
Adoption & enablement
A lean central hub orchestrating a federated champions network embedded in the divisions. Authority from mandate, not headcount.
The record

Atlassian community is a champions network run at enterprise scale: a small central team set the standard and the cadence, and champions embedded in the user base supplied the reach. 78M+ MAU. Users engaged in community were 7x more likely to repurchase. CMX Community of the Year, 2019 and 2020.

My authority there was earned the way this posting says it must be: through business cases, not org charts. Four separate funding wins, including outside consultancy to pressure-test replatform decisions before committing spend.

The mandate
Experience & design research
Measurement shows what is happening; design research explains why colleagues route around the governed path.
The record

Best Offer began with research into how buyers and sellers feel about negotiation; the feature was a behavior change, not a form. When colleagues route around the governed path, the path is telling us something, and someone has to go find out what.

My published practice holds the same line for the AI era: AI surfaces. Humans conclude. Every machine-generated insight links back to its source. People adopt what they trust, and trust is built into the experience, not announced to it.

The mandate
Adoption measurement & value realization
Governed-path usage as the steering signal that drives prioritization, not a downstream report card. Realized value, not launch activity.
The record

My metrics land on the P&L rather than on a usage curve: 7x more likely to repurchase, not sessions. $40M revenue in year one on a 0→1 platform, not signups. Adoption tells us what colleagues tried. Outcomes tell us what worked. The distance between those two numbers is where this role earns its seat.

§ 03The point of view

The leanest team must be the proof.

The posting says the team's leverage is judgment and orchestration, not volume. I published the same argument as the strategist as conductor: the human's job is not to approve AI's output at the end, it is to direct the work while it happens. That is what a four-function core directing a federated network actually does all day. The champions are the orchestra. The hub conducts.

And a hub this lean keeps up only one way: it runs AI-native itself. AI watches governed-path and shadow-path signals across divisions, contextualizes friction, drafts each champion's brief for the week. Humans conclude, prioritize, and carry the relationships. The team accountable for whether colleagues adopt AI should be the enterprise's clearest demonstration of what adoption looks like. This is not hypothetical for me: my methodology is published, my simulation is live, and both were built this way.

One more conviction, for a regulated enterprise: the governed path is not the brake on adoption. Treated as a product, governance is the trust surface, the reason a colleague can rely on what the platform gives them. The accelerator and the brake stay separate, and the accelerator's case is made on merit.

The close
Colleagues do not adopt mandates. They adopt what works for them, and for the people they serve.

The posting asks for a leader who believes the governed path wins because it is the better path. My whole career is a version of that belief: I build places people want to be, not just tools people have to use.
